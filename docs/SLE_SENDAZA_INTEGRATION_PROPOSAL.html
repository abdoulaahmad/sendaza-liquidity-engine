Integration Proposal

Sendaza Liquidity Engine

Multi-asset purchase pricing, MPC-secured treasury custody, and external blockchain withdrawals for Sendaza.

Prepared for: Sendaza

Initial environment: Sandbox with configuration-driven assets, fiat currencies, and networks

Custody: Fireblocks Developer Sandbox using MPC

Status: Design approval and integration planning

Date: 29 August 2026

1. Executive Summary

Sendaza manages what customers own. SLE prices, reserves, safeguards, and delivers the crypto that backs that ownership.

The Sendaza Liquidity Engine (SLE) is a private service that enables Sendaza customers to purchase configured crypto assets using configured fiat balances and withdraw supported assets over approved blockchain networks.

Sendaza remains the customer and accounting system of record. It owns authentication, KYC, transaction authorization, balance locking, and double-entry ledger entries. SLE owns market-data conversion, executable quotes, spreads, purchase fees, treasury availability, inventory reservations, MPC custody integration, blockchain execution, confirmation monitoring, and treasury reconciliation.

The first deliverable serves Sendaza only and runs with test balances, testnet assets, and a Fireblocks Developer Sandbox. External client onboarding and public partner APIs are deferred. The sandbox is not approved for mainnet or real customer funds.

2. Business Deliverable

The sandbox MVP will demonstrate one complete configured crypto/fiat journey:

  • Request a short-lived executable quote for an enabled crypto/fiat market.
  • Purchase the quoted crypto asset using fiat locked in Sendaza.
  • Record the customer's crypto ownership in the Sendaza ledger.
  • Reserve matching asset-network inventory from the SLE treasury.
  • Withdraw the asset to a verified address on an enabled network.
  • Sign and broadcast the withdrawal through Fireblocks MPC custody.
  • Reconcile Sendaza, SLE, Fireblocks, and blockchain records.

OUT OF SCOPE Customer crypto deposits, crypto selling, swaps, staking, lending, automated treasury trading, and mainnet operation.

4. Purchase Flow

Customer selects an enabled crypto/fiat market and enters a fiat amount → Sendaza checks eligibility → Sendaza requests an executable quote from SLE → SLE evaluates the configured conversion route, spread, fees, limits, and sellable inventory → Customer confirms before quote expiry → Sendaza locks the exact fiat debit → SLE reserves the quoted crypto amount on its configured backing network → Sendaza posts balanced fiat and crypto ledger entries → Sendaza acknowledges settlement to SLE → Both systems mark the purchase complete

No blockchain transaction occurs during a purchase. The asset remains in the controlled treasury; the Sendaza ledger records customer ownership, and SLE reduces unallocated inventory by the same amount.

Pricing engine

SLE owns the complete, immutable quote calculation. Pricing routes are configuration-driven rather than hardcoded to ETH or NGN. Examples include BTC/USDT × USDT/USD × USD/NGN, SOL/USD × USD/GHS, or a reliable direct market. SLE stores every source rate, timestamp, conversion step, spread, fee, configuration version, and final customer amount. Sendaza displays the result and must not recalculate it.

5. Withdrawal Flow

Customer enters amount and Ethereum address → SLE validates the destination and estimates fees → Customer confirms the total debit → Sendaza locks the withdrawal asset and applicable fees → SLE evaluates limits, risk, treasury, and gas → SLE submits one transfer instruction to Fireblocks → Fireblocks policy approves automatically or requests review → Fireblocks MPC signs and broadcasts → SLE verifies the transaction through Alchemy → Sendaza finalizes the ledger deduction after required confirmations

A submission timeout is not treated as a failure. Funds remain locked while SLE resolves the provider transfer using the original unique reference.

6. MPC Custody Model

Fireblocks creates and protects the treasury wallet using multi-party computation. SLE never receives or reconstructs the wallet's private key. SLE sends authenticated transfer instructions and receives provider transfer IDs and blockchain transaction hashes.

Fireblocks API authentication credentials are held in encrypted deployment secrets. These credentials allow SLE to request operations but are not the treasury wallet key.

Approval policy

  • Low-risk withdrawals within approved limits may be processed automatically.
  • High-value, unusual, or elevated-risk withdrawals require manual review or multiple approvals.
  • Blocked destinations, insufficient treasury, invalid locks, or unavailable required screening stop the withdrawal.
  • Fireblocks policies independently restrict source wallets, assets, networks, destinations, and amounts.

7. Sendaza Changes Required

  1. Asset precision: support each configured fiat and crypto precision using atomic units or an approved exact representation.
  2. Asset model: distinguish asset from network. For example, USDT on Ethereum, Tron, Solana, and Polygon are separate treasury and withdrawal routes.
  3. Ledger locking: represent available-to-locked movements with balanced, immutable journals.
  4. Settlement protocol: support durable lock, settle, release, and reconciliation states.
  5. SLE client module: sign authenticated requests and attach idempotency and correlation identifiers.
  6. Inbound events: verify SLE webhook signatures, timestamps, and replay identifiers before processing.
  7. Durable messaging: write financial state and outbound acknowledgements into a transactional outbox.
  8. Operation records: retain SLE order ID, client reference, lock reference, settlement reference, and transaction hashes.
  9. Liability reporting: submit ledger-derived, cutoff-aligned liability snapshots for every supported asset.
  10. Provider separation: remove direct crypto pricing, signing, and broadcasting from Sendaza once SLE is integrated.

Customer ownership may be displayed as USDT, but SLE tracks available inventory, token contracts, provider wallets, fees, and limits separately for every supported network. Inventory on one chain cannot fund a withdrawal on another chain until a confirmed rebalancing operation has completed.

9. Failure and Safety Rules

  • A quote is not a purchase and does not reserve treasury inventory.
  • A duplicate request cannot reserve or withdraw funds twice.
  • An expired quote cannot be accepted.
  • A provider timeout after submission does not prove failure.
  • A broadcast transaction is not considered confirmed.
  • Funds may be automatically released only after proven failure before broadcast.
  • Uncertain results remain locked and enter reconciliation.
  • Completed financial records remain immutable; corrections use compensating records.

  • Approve the Sendaza/SLE ownership boundary.
  • Approve the asset registry, precision rules, and ledger-account migration.
  • Approve journal-backed locking, settlement, and reversal behavior.
  • Decide purchase spread and explicit fee presentation.
  • Decide withdrawal network-fee treatment and variance ownership.
  • Approve automatic withdrawal limits and manual-review thresholds.
  • Approve confirmation requirements by transaction value.
  • Approve the liability snapshot definition and reporting cadence.
  • Nominate engineering, treasury, compliance, and operations owners.

12. Acceptance Criteria

The sandbox integration is accepted when:

  • A test customer buys a configured crypto asset using locked test fiat.
  • Sendaza records exact, balanced, immutable ledger entries.
  • SLE reserves sufficient asset-network inventory without overselling the treasury.
  • The customer withdraws a supported testnet asset through Fireblocks MPC custody.
  • Sendaza finalizes the deduction only after required confirmations.
  • Duplicate, timeout, delayed-webhook, and restart tests do not duplicate or lose value.
  • Sendaza, SLE, Fireblocks, and blockchain evidence reconcile at a common cutoff.
